Polar plots and the Nyquist condition

✨AI-generated recap — check anything important against the slides

The root locus answers "is the closed loop stable?" by moving poles around. Nyquist answers the same question from the open-loop frequency response alone, and it also says how close you are to the edge. That distance is what gain and phase margin measure.

The polar plot

  • Draw G(jω) as a single curve in the complex plane, with ω running along it. Sketching the Bode plot first is the easiest way to get it right.
  • Only two places really matter: where the curve crosses the unit circle (|G| = 1) and where it crosses the negative real axis (phase −180°).
  • The slides go through the integrator (the negative imaginary axis), a single pole (a half-circle from 1 to 0), a complex pair (down to −180°), and a longer transfer function.

The idea behind it: counting by angles

  • Walk s once around a closed curve Γ. Each factor (s − z) or (s − p) is an arrow from that zero or pole to s, and ∠G is the sum of the zero angles minus the pole angles.
  • An arrow from a point outside Γ swings back and forth and ends where it started: a net change of 0. An arrow from a point inside Γ goes all the way round: ±360°.
  • So the number of times G(s) circles the origin is N = Z − P: the zeros minus the poles inside Γ. This is the principle of the argument.

From that to closed-loop stability

  • The closed-loop poles are the zeros of 1 + kL(s). The poles of 1 + kL(s) are just the poles of L(s).
  • Choose Γ as the Nyquist contour, or D-contour: up the imaginary axis and round a huge half-circle enclosing the right half-plane.
  • Then 1 + kL circling 0 is the same as L circling the point −1/k. For k = 1 that is the famous −1 point.
  • On the imaginary axis L(−jω) is the mirror image of L(jω), so the Nyquist plot is just the polar plot plus its reflection in the real axis.

The Nyquist condition

Let P be the number of unstable open-loop poles and N the number of clockwise encirclements of −1/k. The closed loop then has Z = N + P unstable poles. If L is stable (P = 0), the closed loop is stable as long as the plot does not circle −1/k. If L has P unstable poles, the plot has to circle −1/k exactly P times counter-clockwise.

Poles on the imaginary axis

  • The contour cannot go through a pole, so make a tiny indentation around each pole on the axis, leaving it on the left.
  • On the plot that small detour turns into a huge arc at infinity. Close it the right way: if you went counter-clockwise round the pole, close the plot clockwise.
  • The slides work through 4/(s(s + 2)), (s − 2)/s² and 2/((s² + 1)(s + 1)).

How far from the edge: margins

Gain margin

At ω180, where the phase is −180°: GM = 1/|L(jω180)|. How much the gain may grow before the plot hits −1. On the Bode plot: the gap below 0 dB at ω180.

Phase margin

At the crossover ωc, where |L| = 1: φm = 180° + ∠L(jωc). How much extra phase lag the loop can take. On the Bode plot: the gap above −180° at ωc.

  • For a stable, minimum-phase L, Nyquist on the Bode plot becomes: the magnitude must be below 0 dB where the phase crosses −180°, and the phase above −180° where the magnitude crosses 0 dB.
  • If L is unstable or has right-half-plane zeros, don't trust that shortcut. Check with the full Nyquist plot or the root locus.

Specs in the frequency domain

  • References and disturbances live at low frequencies, sensor noise at high ones. That is how you get around S + T = 1: make each one small where it matters.
  • Tracking: |S| small at low frequency, which means |L| large there. Written as |L(jω)| > |W1(jω)|, a low-frequency obstacle on the Bode plot.
  • Noise rejection: |T| small at high frequency, which means |L| small there. Written as |L(jω)| < 1/|W2(jω)|, a high-frequency obstacle.
  • In between, cross 0 dB with a slope of about −20 dB/decade to keep a good phase margin. The crossover ωc is roughly the closed-loop bandwidth.
  • Loop shaping is threading |L| through this obstacle course.

The closed loop, seen from L

  • |T| ≈ 1 where |L| ≫ 1: the output follows the reference.
  • |T| ≈ |L| where |L| ≪ 1: noise is passed on only as much as L lets it through.
  • |T| ≫ 1 near L ≈ −1: a resonance peak, and the sign of a poor margin.
