Introduction

✨AI-generated recap — check anything important against the slides

Machines got complicated enough that a human could no longer hold them at the behaviour we wanted. Control is the business of getting them to hold it themselves.

The idea

  • First came tools, then machines with their own power source. Once the operator was no longer supplying the force, something had to decide how the machine should act.
  • That something is a controller: it holds the system at a reference the operator sets, in spite of disturbances.
  • Control is nearly invisible — a car has dozens of controllers in it — and mostly gets noticed when it fails.

Three architectures

Open loop

The command is set without ever measuring the result. A disturbance goes uncorrected.

Closed loop

The output is measured and compared with the reference. The error drives the command.

Two degrees of freedom

Feedback for the errors, plus a direct path from the reference, tuned separately.

What a controller is asked to do

  • Stabilization — keep the plant from running away from the desired behaviour.
  • Performance — carry out the task as accurately as possible.
  • Robustness — still do both under disturbances, modelling errors and measurement noise.

What the course will do with it

  • Model, analyse, design — the three objectives, applied to systems described by ODEs that are linear, time-invariant, and single-input single-output.
  • Much of it happens with complex numbers in the frequency domain rather than in time. The slides call this a leap of faith, and warn the curve is steep.
  • By the end you should be able to design a controller for the quadcopter lateral dynamics, and explain why it works.

If you keep one thing

Feedback gets you the behaviour you want even when the model is wrong and the world pushes back — but it can also introduce instabilities that were not there before. Both halves of that sentence are the course.

Worth doing this week

  • Read Åström & Murray, chapter 1 — free online, and the suggested reading for this lecture.
  • Try the speed control game from the slides, then the quadcopter altitude one. Losing at them is the point.
  • In the exercise session, step through the loop and watch an open loop drift away.
